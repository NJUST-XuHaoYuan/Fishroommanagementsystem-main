# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are terminal consumers browsing marine ornamental livestock before purchase. They are likely opening the page from a shared link, social profile, or customer service message, and they need to quickly understand what species are available, which products match that species, and whether individual animals have credible maintenance history.

Secondary users are store staff who maintain the internal fishroom system. Their operational data is the source of truth, but the consumer surface must stay read-only and avoid exposing backend-only information such as customer records, orders, staff accounts, procurement costs, and internal notes that are not meant for buyers.

## Product Purpose

The consumer catalog turns the fishroom management system into a public, trust-building storefront. Consumers can search by species, compare available products, inspect individual stock status, and read maintenance records so they feel confident asking to reserve or buy a specific animal.

Success means the public page feels premium and reliable, loads from the existing system data without manual duplicate entry, and presents livestock care evidence without leaking private operational data.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, boutique. The tone should feel like a high-end marine livestock buyer's room: expert and visually refined, with enough restraint that the animals and records remain the focus.

## Anti-references

Do not make this look like a generic admin dashboard, wholesale spreadsheet, low-end marketplace listing page, or overdecorated aquarium fan site. Avoid fake metrics, heavy dark-blue SaaS styling, generic card grids, neon reef cliches, and any layout that makes maintenance records feel like marketing copy rather than observed history.

## Design Principles

1. Evidence before persuasion: maintenance timelines, dates, condition labels, and item availability should carry more weight than slogans.
2. Public by design: only expose data that a buyer should see; never leak customer, order, staff, account, cost, or internal permission data.
3. Species-first browsing: the main mental model is finding a species, then narrowing to products and individual available animals.
4. Boutique restraint: use refined imagery, typography, and spacing to create a premium feel without turning the page into a decorative landing page.
5. Operational truth: visible catalog state should derive from the current system, including sold, shipped, lost, and in-tank status.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Support keyboard search and selection, clear focus states, reduced-motion preferences, readable Chinese labels, and responsive layouts for mobile consumers opening links from chat or social apps.
